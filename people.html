These introductions are based on linked records. They are not complete biographies, and no generated portrait is used to represent a historical person.

01

A home in Alexandra / 1942

Nelson Mandela

The architectural account of the Alexandra Heritage Centre identifies a dwelling in Mandela’s Yard where Nelson Mandela lived in 1942.

A small home can connect a widely known life to a particular neighbourhood.

02

A participant remembers / 1957

Roseinnes Phahle

In an essay written in 2019, Phahle recalls growing up in Alexandra and participating in the 1957 bus boycott.

He explains that he later consulted newspaper reports to compare them with his memories. His account invites attention to people whose work may receive less recognition.

A retrospective personal account, read alongside other evidence.

03

Testimony and community memory / 1996

Bennet Lekalakala

At the Alexandra TRC hearing on 29 October 1996, Lekalakala gave an account of political struggle, youth activity and conflict in the township during the 1970s and 1980s.

The transcript records witness testimony, rather than a final finding for each statement. It contains accounts of violence.

Culture leaves its own record

Music, creativity
and recognition.

The 2012 centenary address associates Alexandra with performers including Simon “Mahlathini” Nkabinde, Ntemi Piliso and the Dark City Sisters.

These names open another route into the archive: music, performance and the people who shape a place’s cultural memory.
