Alexandra through time

A place in time.
A story still unfolding.

Follow selected moments in Alexandra’s past, and the people whose actions and memories connect them.

This is a selected chronology, not a complete history. Each entry includes a source and context. No generated illustration is presented as documentary evidence.

Beginnings / 1912 Collective action / 1957–1986 Living memory / 2012 onward
1912 Beginnings

A township takes shape

Plots were sold to African and Coloured buyers with freehold rights in 1912. This is the year commemorated in Alexandra’s centenary.

Read context and source

The 2012 centenary address describes the sale of plots and the significance of freehold ownership. It is an official commemorative account, read here as one source rather than an exhaustive history.

Presidency: centenary address (22 September 2012) ↗ (opens in a new tab)

1957 Collective action

Walking together

Residents boycotted buses in opposition to higher fares. The campaign forms an important chapter in Alexandra’s history of collective action.

Read context and source

Participant Roseinnes Phahle’s 2019 essay reflects on organising, public meetings and the people involved. His account combines personal recollection with research; it offers a perspective on the boycott, rather than a neutral record of every event.

Roseinnes Phahle: participant account (June 2019) ↗ (opens in a new tab)

1976 Collective action

Young people take to the streets

In testimony to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, Bennet Lekalakala described students and young people in Alexandra joining the wider 1976 uprising.

Read context and source

Lekalakala’s hearing testimony links youth mobilisation in Alexandra to the uprising and describes confrontation with state forces. The account was given in 1996, two decades after those events.

TRC: Bennet Lekalakala testimony (29 October 1996) ↗ (opens in a new tab)

1986 Collective action

The Six Day War

Lekalakala’s testimony describes the violence known as the Six Day War and its impact on community life in Alexandra.

Read context and source

This entry concerns deaths, mourning and political violence. The testimony discusses events surrounding a funeral and the ensuing conflict. It is presented as an attributed witness account, not a substitute for the Commission’s final findings.

TRC: Bennet Lekalakala testimony (29 October 1996) ↗ (opens in a new tab)

2012 Living memory

A century remembered

On 22 September 2012, a presidential address marked Alexandra’s centenary and reflected on its people, cultural life and struggle heritage.

Read context and source

The address situates the centenary within public remembrance of Alexandra. It is also a political speech; the historical information should be considered alongside other sources.

Presidency: centenary address (22 September 2012) ↗ (opens in a new tab)
