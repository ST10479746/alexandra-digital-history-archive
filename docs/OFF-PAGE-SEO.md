## Purpose and audience

Help people interested in Alexandra township, Johannesburg, discover an educational website covering local history, landmarks, community memory and historical references. Initial audiences include fellow students, educators, people connected with Alexandra and readers interested in South African heritage.

Describe it consistently as an educational student project. It is not the official website of the Alexandra Heritage Centre. Illustrative AI media must not be promoted as original documentary photography.

## 1. Relevant backlinks

A backlink is an incoming link from another website. Adding links to historical sources on this archive is useful referencing, but does not by itself create incoming backlinks.

### Candidate routes

| Route | Why it fits | Concrete action after launch | Evidence | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Creator's existing portfolio or public student-project profile, if available | Helps interested readers discover the project | Add a clearly labelled link to the verified live site | Public page URL and dated screenshot | Planned; no profile availability assumed |
| A lecturer-managed class project showcase, if one exists and inclusion is permitted | Reaches students and educators in a relevant setting | Ask its editor whether the finished student project is suitable | Outreach record; published linking page if accepted | Planned; no approval or listing claimed |
| An independently maintained Alexandra/community-history resource | Readers have a direct interest in the subject | Identify a relevant editor and offer the project for editorial review, including its educational and AI-media context | Request date, response and actual public linking page if accepted | Planned; no partnership or acceptance claimed |

These are prospective routes, not established contacts. Verify the resource, correct contact and submission rules before outreach. Editors control whether they link; a request is not an acquired backlink. Record a declined or unanswered request honestly.

Prefer a small number of relevant editorial links over unrelated directory entries. Do not buy links, arrange ranking-driven exchanges or publish repetitive promotional comments. A profile or social link may be marked nofollow/ugc or may not be crawlable; count it as discovery/promotion evidence rather than promising ranking benefits.

## 2. Social promotion

After deployment, publish one project introduction using an account the creator already controls. A public LinkedIn or Facebook post may suit the academic/community context. Do not create a new account merely to populate this plan.

A second, distinct post can introduce the sourced timeline or interactive landmarks map. Use a page-specific live link, a suitable website screenshot and a brief description of what visitors can do.

Share with a community group only when its rules and administrators permit project promotion. A private WhatsApp status or private class-group share can demonstrate communication, but is not an independently verifiable public backlink.

Use the drafts in PROMOTION-DRAFTS.md. They are not scheduled or published. Avoid claiming institutional endorsement, completed community partnerships, collected resident testimony or measured public impact.

Social activity is a way to reach readers and invite feedback. Likes and shares are not proof of improved search rankings.

## 3. Local relevance

Use a consistent name: Alexandra Digital History Archive.

Use an accurate location description: Alexandra township, Johannesburg, Gauteng, South Africa.

Mention actual subjects where relevant: Alexandra history, community memory, Alexandra Heritage Centre and the interactive landmarks map. Link interested readers to the corresponding page rather than adding a list of unrelated place names.

Do not create a business listing, publish a fictional street address, invent visiting hours or use the Heritage Centre's address as the student's own. The website does not claim to operate a public museum premises.

The source links on the Research and Places pages establish research context; they do not imply endorsement by the publishers.

## 4. Implementation and evidence

Complete these actions after the deployment in the agreed final stage:

1. Open the public Home page and one exhibition URL on another device. Confirm the links work before sharing.
2. Publish one approved social introduction and save its public URL, publication date and a screenshot.
3. Add a website link to an existing creator-controlled profile or portfolio, if available, and record its URL.
4. Identify one relevant independent educational or heritage resource. Verify the intended contact and request editorial consideration. Record the request separately from any eventual published link.
5. Check any claimed backlink by opening the external page and following its link back to the live archive.
6. Update off-page-evidence.csv with the actual outcome. Do not mark a planned or requested link as published.

Screenshots used for submission should show the relevant project post or link. Exclude private conversations, account details and other people's personal information unless there is a legitimate need and permission to include them.
